# Cheap Poboys, Fewer Shops: Understanding the Economic Squeeze on New Orleans Restaurants

For generations, New Orleans poboys have been famous for being affordable. But a quiet shift is underway: fewer poboy shops are opening, and some are closing.

## Why?

It comes down to the economics of the burger. Poboy shops sell high-volume, low-priced meals. But the cost of running a shop — labor, ingredients, rent, insurance, health compliance — keeps climbing. When those costs rise faster than the price point, the shop loses money.

The shop owner faces a choice: raise prices, cut corners, or close. Many choose to close.

## The Broader Trend

This isn't unique to New Orleans. Across the food industry, rising operating costs are squeezing small restaurants. But poboy shops are especially vulnerable because they sell high-volume, low-margin meals, rely on labor-intensive preparation, often sit in historic buildings with high rent and insurance, and serve price-sensitive customers who can easily switch to a cheaper alternative.

## What This Means for the Culture

Poboy shops aren't just businesses. They're gathering places, community anchors, and culinary institutions. When they close, the city loses a part of its identity. The food culture that made New Orleans famous begins to thin out.

## How to Push Back

1. **Value over price.** Pay for the craft, not just the price.
2. **Support local.** Independent shops need your business.
3. **Normalize quality.** A cheap poboy shouldn't mean a poorly made one.
4. **Advocate.** Support policies that keep small restaurants viable.
